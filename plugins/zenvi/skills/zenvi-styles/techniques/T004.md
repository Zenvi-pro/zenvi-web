# T004 Mesh-pinned HUD callouts

## What it is

A 3D object turns slowly while thin anchor rings sit on points of its mesh,
each with a leader line to a 2 % mono readout (`X 0.740 Y 0.210`), and one
numeral at 35-45 % of frame height counts up beside it. The spec beat of a
product or engine CGI film: one claim, one number, the object proving it.

## Build it in Zenvi

Zenvi cannot yet pin a callout to a moving mesh point (the 3D render returns
no projected anchors), so pick anchors that do not move on screen: points on
the turntable axis (top cap centre, base centre) and the silhouette of a
round object. Turn it 60-90° at most, so any other point drifts under 2 %.

Render the object on pure black (`#000000`, the same as the project; any
other dark shows the scaled render's box), shrink it and push it to the
right third:

```json
add_3d_scene_tool {"scene": "model_showcase", "quality": "draft", "position_seconds": 0, "duration_seconds": 4, "track": "1", "params": {"model": "<model.glb, or product: speaker>", "move": "turntable", "turn_degrees": 90, "stage": "dark", "look": "wireframe", "background": "#000000", "accent": "#39d0ff", "elevation": 12}}
set_clip_properties_tool {"timeline_clip_ids": ["<3d clip>"], "properties": {"scale_x": 0.78, "scale_y": 0.78, "location_x": 0.24}}
```

Render the result and read the anchors off a gridded frame (ffmpeg
`drawgrid`, or `preview_frame` where the surface has it) at the middle of
the turn. Readouts go in `labels` (small mono), not `values` (those are set
at about 4 %, too big for this look):

```json
add_motion_block_tool {"block": "hud_callouts", "position_seconds": 0.3, "duration_seconds": 3.7, "placement": "over_video", "params": {"title": "UNIT 04 / SCAN", "labels": ["DRIVER  X 0.740  Y 0.210", "PORT  X 0.612  Y 0.500", "BASE  X 0.740  Y 0.765"], "values": [], "points": [{"x": 0.74, "y": 0.21}, {"x": 0.612, "y": 0.5}, {"x": 0.74, "y": 0.765}], "grid": "crosshair", "palette": "night", "accent": "#39d0ff", "display_font": "mono"}}
add_motion_block_tool {"block": "counter", "position_seconds": 0.6, "duration_seconds": 3.4, "placement": "over_video", "params": {"values": ["0", "40"], "suffix": "h", "label": "PLAYBACK", "style": "odometer", "align": "left", "weight": 800, "palette": "night", "accent": "#39d0ff"}}
set_clip_properties_tool {"timeline_clip_ids": ["<counter clip>"], "properties": {"scale_x": 1.5, "scale_y": 1.5, "location_x": 0.15}}
fade_clip_tool {"clip_id": "<each of the three clips>", "fade_out_seconds": 0.5}
```

The counter's figure is about 25 % of frame height as rendered; scale 1.5
brings two digits plus a unit to about 37 %. Check the look in draft, then
`rerender_3d_scene_tool {"timeline_clip_id": "<3d clip>", "quality": "final"}`.

Fallback for an object that is not round, or a faster turn: hold the turn
under 30° and keep anchors near the axis, or cut the callouts in only after
the object stops (`turn_degrees` small, callouts from the last second).

## Timing & taste rules

- Numeral 35-45 % of frame height, readouts 1.5-2.5 %: the contrast is the
  look. Two digits and a unit fit beside the object; three digits push it
  off the third.
- 3-4 s per spec beat. Rings pop about 0.6-1.6 s in; the count lands by
  1.8 s and holds.
- Anchors drift at most 1-2 % of frame over the beat; anything more reads as
  a label sliding off the part.
- Do not put a readout over the brightest part of the mesh: labels in the
  right column overlap a wide object, so keep the object at 0.78 scale.
- `look: "wireframe"` with `background: "transparent"` rendered as a flat
  white body in this test: use a solid black background instead.

## Sound for it

Dry and small: a glitch tick as the grid draws, a short rise into the first
count, one tap per ring (-18 dB), fast key ticks while the digits roll
(about 6 per second, -22 dB), a pop as the number lands, a room-tone bed at
-30 dB and a short bloom tail at the end. No music hit; this is a data beat.

## Common mistakes

- Pinning rings to points that swing round with the turn: they float off.
- Readouts as big as headlines (`values`), which flattens the scale contrast.
- A dark-grey render background on a black project: the box shows.
- Ending on the hold with no fade or tail (the judge's first complaint).

pins hold only because the anchors sit on the rotation axis. True pinning
to any vertex of a turning model needs `mesh-anchored-callouts-3d`
(projected anchor positions from the Blender camera).
