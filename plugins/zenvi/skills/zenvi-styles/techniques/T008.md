# T008 Surface-locked perspective type

## What it is

A stat or claim (7-12 % of frame height) that lies on a surface of the shot,
the floor or desk in front of the product or the chassis, snapping in letter
by letter and held 1.7-4 s. It replaces the centred lower third in laptop and
device launch films.

## Build it in Zenvi

Zenvi has no planar tracker or corner pin, so the type is squashed onto the
surface with clip scale, which is only right for a locked or slowly pushing
shot that looks at the surface straight on (a desk or floor seen frontally,
a top-down deck). Pick that shot; leave the camera-moving ones alone.

```json
add_clip_to_timeline_tool {"file_id": "<laptop on desk>", "position_seconds": 0, "start_seconds": 5.0, "end_seconds": 9.5, "track": "1"}
add_motion_block_tool {"block": "kinetic_headline", "position_seconds": 0.4, "duration_seconds": 3.6, "params": {"text": "18 HRS", "style": "pop", "position": "lower", "max_size": 230, "ink": "#1c1c1c", "display_font": "display", "exit": true, "palette": "light"}}
set_clip_properties_tool {"timeline_clip_ids": ["<headline>"], "properties": {"scale_y": 0.45, "scale_x": 1.1, "location_y": 0.235, "alpha": 0.9}}
add_effect_tool {"timeline_clip_id": "<headline>", "effect": "Shadow", "properties": {"angle": 90, "distance": 6, "blur_radius": 20, "opacity": 0.2}}
fade_clip_tool {"clip_id": "<laptop on desk>", "fade_out_seconds": 0.3}
```

Scale works about the frame centre (`origin_y` does not move the pivot for
scale), so after squashing, place the line with `location_y`: new centre =
`0.5 + (y - 0.5) * scale_y + location_y`. Here the headline sits at 0.8,
squashed to 0.45 it would land at 0.635, so +0.235 puts it at 0.87 on the
desk. Keep it in front of the product, never on it.

Use one motion block, not `add_kinetic_title_tool`: the kinetic title is one
clip per letter, each with its own scale keyframes, and squashing them flattens
their animation. On a top-down shot (keyboard deck facing the lens) skip the
squash and only `rotation` the block to the deck's angle.

Fallback for a moving camera: put the stat on a black ground shot (or a
`title_3d` over a dark backdrop) and say the type is not locked to the scene.

## Timing & taste rules

- Height on screen 7-12 % after the squash (230 px max size at 0.45 is about
  6 %; go to 260-300 for a short stat).
- Squash 0.4-0.5 for a desk seen from just above its height; 0.7-0.8 for a
  floor seen from standing height.
- In by 0.4 s into the shot, letters at the block's pop stagger, hold 2-3 s,
  exit animated (`exit: true`), and cut 0.3-0.5 s after it leaves.
- Ink close to the surface's own dark (#1c1c1c on white, alpha 0.9) and a
  wide, faint contact shadow (blur 20, opacity 0.2). A 10 px, 0.35 shadow was
  called "muddy" and read as a bevel.

## Sound for it

A `pop` (-14 dB) as the first letter lands and a low `impact_thud` pitch -3
(-12 dB) when the stat settles, a short whoosh on the exit. A dry
`hit_transient` alone was judged harsh. Low drone underneath; the stat gets
the only hit in its shot.

## Common mistakes

- Squashing the stat on a shot where the camera orbits or the surface turns:
  the type slides against the floor.
- Forgetting that scale moves the line toward the centre.
- Per-letter kinetic titles squashed one by one.
- A static hold with no exit, then a hard cut (the judge's first fix both
  times).

## Verified

2026-10-28. Pexels clip of a laptop on a sunlit white desk, 4.5 s at
1920x1080: render, contact sheet and judge in
`~/zenvi-motion/fleet/artifacts/sk2-product-brand/techniques/T008.*`. Pass 1
(stat from 0.8 s, no shadow, `hit_transient` click, hard cut) 2.9/10; pass 2
(starts at 0.4 s, exit on, Shadow effect, `pop` + `impact_thud`) 4.0/10; the
judge said the planar match "reads convincingly", then marked down the dark
10 px shadow (softened in the recipe above, not re-judged), the frozen hold
and the blank laptop screen. No perspective convergence: the near edge of the
type is no wider than the far edge, so it only passes on frontal shots. The
faithful version needs `planar-surface-track-overlay` (corner pin plus planar
track), and a light sweep across the letters, which nothing does today.
