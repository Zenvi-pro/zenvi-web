# T025 Single-accent palette grade

## What it is

Each sequence gets its own held-down grade (cold teal night, steel grey,
sand) and keeps exactly one saturated colour: red rims on cyan fog, amber
lamps on grey, yellow on basalt. Fine grain and halation on lights, cut in a
2.33-2.39:1 band. It shines on car films and trailers, where the palette
changes by act and the accent is the product.

## Build it in Zenvi

Zenvi has no hue-isolate grade (keep one hue, desaturate the rest), so the
accent has to be the only strongly saturated thing in the source: pick shots
where it already is (coloured rims, amber lamp rings, a red coat on a grey
street). A global saturation of 0.7-0.8 then mutes the surround more than the
accent. Do not use negative `vibrance` to protect it: at -0.9 it greyed the
accents along with everything else.

```json
add_clip_to_timeline_tool {"file_id": "<night shot, red rims>", "position_seconds": 0, "start_seconds": 8.5, "end_seconds": 12.7, "track": "1"}
add_clip_to_timeline_tool {"file_id": "<grey-day shot, amber lamps>", "position_seconds": 4.2, "start_seconds": 1.6, "end_seconds": 5.8, "track": "1"}
color_grade_clip_tool {"timeline_clip_id": "<seq A>", "reset": true, "saturation": 0.8, "contrast": 0.15, "temperature": -0.25, "tint": -0.05, "exposure": 0.1, "shadows": 0.2, "curves": {"all": [[0, 0.03], [0.25, 0.24], [0.75, 0.8], [1, 1]]}}
color_grade_clip_tool {"timeline_clip_id": "<seq B>", "reset": true, "saturation": 0.75, "contrast": 0.2, "temperature": -0.12, "exposure": 0.15}
apply_fx_tool {"clip_id": "<seq B>", "effects": [{"effect": "bloom"}]}
apply_look_preset_tool {"timeline_clip_ids": ["<seq A>", "<seq B>"], "look": "film_grain_35mm_fine"}
add_shape_mask_tool {"clip_ids": ["<seq A>", "<seq B>"], "shape": "rectangle", "width": 1.2, "height": 0.744, "feather": 0}
add_vignette_tool {"clip_ids": ["<seq A>", "<seq B>"], "amount": 0.3, "feather": 0.6}
```

The mask height is 16:9 / 2.39 = 0.744 (0.763 for 2.33:1); feather 0 gives
hard black bars. Mute camera sound (`set_clip_volume_tool` `level_db` -60 per
clip) and score the cut:

```json
add_sfx_tool {"recipe": "whoosh_rise", "at_seconds": 4.2, "duration": 1.0, "gain_db": -18}
add_sfx_tool {"recipe": "impact_boom", "at_seconds": 4.2, "pitch": -5, "gain_db": -10}
fade_clip_tool {"clip_id": "<last shot>", "fade_out_seconds": 1.2}
master_audio_tool {"preset": "social", "iterate": true}
```

Fallback when the accent is not isolated in the source: pick another shot.
Grading a busy, many-coloured frame down to "one accent" is not possible today.

## Timing & taste rules

- One accent per sequence, the same object or colour for the whole
  sequence; a palette change only on a chapter cut (2-4 shots per palette).
- Surround saturation 0.7-0.8, never below 0.6 (the accent dies with it).
  Shift temperature by sequence (-0.25 night teal, -0.1 steel).
- Lift the toe (curve point 0 → 0.03, shadows +0.2) on night footage: a
  crushed noisy toe was the judge's first complaint.
- Bloom only on shots whose lamps are not already blown: on a clipped
  headlight it reads as digital clipping (the judge's fix on pass 2).
- Bars 2.39:1, black, for the whole film. Grain 35 mm fine, not gritty.

## Sound for it

A low cinematic drone under the whole piece, entering with the first fade.
Each palette change is a chapter cut: a short rise into a pitched-down boom
on it. Let the music run past the last frame and fade with the picture
(a bed cut at the clip end sounds broken).

## Common mistakes

- Negative vibrance or a black-and-white grade to "isolate" the accent.
- Two saturated colours in one sequence (a red lamp and a blue sign).
- One LUT over every chapter: the palette has to move with the story.
- Bloom on blown highlights; a crushed toe on noisy night stock.
- The music bed ending hard on the last frame.
