# t024 Print and paper texture look

## What it is

One texture over everything: a creased paper layer plus edge darkening and grain makes 17 cards in 17
palettes read as one book; the print variant adds offset colour edges and halftone. It is the cheapest way
to make generated or mixed assets feel designed.

## Build it in Zenvi

1. A paper texture clip over the whole piece on the top video track: your own scan or a generated one (e.g.
   ffmpeg `color=c=#d8ccb0` with `noise=alls=40:allf=t`), `add_clip_to_timeline_tool` `"track": "<top>"`.
2. Blend it by opacity: `set_clip_property_tool` `{"clip_id": "<paper>", "property": "alpha", "value": 0.28}`
   (0.2-0.35; see Missing for real multiply).
3. Grain and edges on the shots: `apply_look_preset_tool` `{"timeline_clip_ids": [...],
   "look": "film_grain_35mm_gritty"}`, `add_vignette_tool` `{"clip_ids": [...], "amount": 0.55}`.
4. Print variant: `color_grade_clip_tool` (contrast up, clipped blacks) and `apply_fx_tool` where available.

## Timing & taste rules

- Texture opacity 0.2-0.35: visible on flat areas, invisible on faces.
- The texture never cuts with the shots; it is one continuous layer.

## Sound for it

Paper and pencil foley on transitions suit it (`add_sfx_tool` `paper`).

## Common mistakes

- Opacity above 0.4: everything turns beige and soft.
- A moving (boiling) texture on fast cuts: keep the grain subtle.
