# t043 Screen-capture and live-action intercut

## What it is

The talk-to-chart cut: the presenter carries the argument in a long hold, and the picture cuts hard to a
full-frame capture (chart, map, screen) for 3-8 s while the data moves, then back to the presenter for the
conclusion. Cuts land on the gesture or on the start of the animation, never on the beat.

## Build it in Zenvi

1. Presenter on track 1: `add_clip_to_timeline_tool` `{"file_id": "<talk>", "position_seconds": 0,
   "start_seconds": 1, "end_seconds": 3.5}`.
2. The capture full frame on track 2 at the cut: a supplied screen recording with `add_clip_to_timeline_tool`,
   or a built one: `add_motion_block_tool` `{"block": "chart_build", "placement": "full_frame", "track": "2",
   "position_seconds": 2.5, "duration_seconds": 4, "params": {"kind": "line", "title": "Life expectancy, years",
   "values": ["48","55","62","68","72"], "labels": ["1950","1970","1990","2010","2020"], "palette": "dark"}}`.
3. Back to the presenter after the capture (`add_clip_to_timeline_tool` with a later source window), or with
   one long take on track 1 just let it run under the capture and use `swap_shot_tool` to change the angle
   without moving the cut.
4. Retime a capture so its fastest movement lands on the line naming it: `set_clip_speed_tool`.
5. A soft cue on each cut: `add_sfx_tool` `{"recipe": "whoosh_short", "at_seconds": <in>}` and
   `{"recipe": "mouse_click", "at_seconds": <out>}`.

## Timing & taste rules

- Presenter holds are long (p90 12-18 s in talks); captures are 3-8 s, never under 2 s if they must be read.
- Cut into the capture within 0.5 s of the chart starting to move, and before the VO names what moves.
- No axis labels should ever have to be read from a wide shot of the screen.

## Sound for it

A quiet whoosh or click on each cut, under the voice (-14 to -18 dB). Voice continuous across both cuts.

## Common mistakes

- Picture-in-picture instead of a full-frame cut: the chart is unreadable on a phone.
- Cutting back before the animation finishes: the viewer never sees the end state.
