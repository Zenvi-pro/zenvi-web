# t124 Sentence-level interview subtitles

## What it is

Plain pop-on subtitles for a subject speaking (often in another language) over footage of their work: one
phrase or sentence at a time, small bold sans at 2.8-4% height, bottom centre, each cue 2.5-5 s. They carry
meaning, not emphasis; the picture keeps the attention.

## Build it in Zenvi

1. Transcript: `transcribe_media_tool` `{"fileId": "<interview>"}` then `add_captions_tool`
   `{"clipId": "<interview clip>", "style": "classic", "maxWords": 12, "text_size": 0.04,
   "background_opacity": 0}`. From a script or translation: `add_captions_tool` `{"cues": [{"start": 0.4,
   "end": 3.6, "text": "My father did this work, and his father before him."}, ...], ...}` or `srtPath`.
2. Restyle or fix a line: `edit_captions_tool`.
3. Cut the picture on sentence ends: `split_clip_tool` / `trim_clip_tool` at the cue boundaries.

## Timing & taste rules

- One phrase per 2.5-5 s; never more than two lines; 2.8-4% height.
- Bottom centre, above the safe margin; white with a thin shadow or outline, no box on cinematic material.
- Cue in on the first syllable, out at the sentence end (not on a fixed duration).

## Sound for it

The voice and the work's own sound; music, if any, 15+ dB under the voice.

## Common mistakes

- Word-by-word kinetic captions on an observational documentary.
- Subtitles on a clip that already carries its own readable speech in the viewer's language (long-form essays).
